Two rhythms, two clocks

Eyes every 20 minutes: look 20 feet away for 20 seconds. Body every 45: long enough to leave the chair. The two are tracked separately, so skipping one never moves the other.

It waits for a good moment

A break only interrupts what can be interrupted. When the last reason clears, Vista waits one more minute — then it lands.

A microphone or camera in use — and it knows which app. A meeting you only listen to still counts.

A break you can see past

Your desktop blurs instead of disappearing. One instruction, something to follow — a dot for near and far, paced blinking, a breathing circle, stepped stretches — and a ring that drains while you do it.

A minute's warning first, with Start now, +1, +5 and +15. Snooze, skip or lock the screen from the break itself; Esc twice always ends one. Make skipping as easy or as hard as you like: Casual, Balanced, Hardcore.

The rest of it

Everything here is off, on or yours to set. Vista is a menu-bar app: no dock icon, no window to keep open.

  • Credit for time away

    Lunch, a meeting in another room, the school run — time away from the keyboard counts as a break. Vista says so, and lets you take it back if it wasn't one.

  • Planned breaks

    Fixed times that are yours whatever the clocks say: lunch at one, a walk at four.

  • Posture and blink nudges

    Shoulders down, screen at eye level, and a reminder to blink — screens halve your blink rate. Both off until you switch them on.

  • Your break, your way

    Blur, your wallpaper, a gradient or your own picture behind it. Your own messages, three chime pairs, a system sound or a file of your own.

  • Shortcuts and automations

    Two global hotkeys, Shortcuts actions, and a Shortcut, AppleScript or shell command of your own on every break start and end.

  • On your wrist With the watch app

    The Mac's break taps your wrist, walking about sixty steps ends a move break and counts it, and Done or Snooze works from the watch.

    Built, not yet verified on a paired watch.

Two shortcuts worth remembering

⌃⌥⌘B takes a break right now. ⌃⌥⌘P pauses Vista, and starts it again.

Type on your keyboard. The keys light up with yours.

A number for the day, one change a fortnight

Screen time, your longest stretch without a break, your typical one, where the time went, and a screen score out of a hundred. Seven days at a glance, and a summary on Friday.

After a fortnight Vista reads your own record back to you and offers one change. Accept it in a click, or leave it alone.
